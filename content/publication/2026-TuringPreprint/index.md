abstract: "The Turing Test is passed when a human interrogator cannot distinguish machine-generated responses from human ones. Yet behavioral indistinguishability can reflect two fundamentally different failures: a failure of encoding, in which machine and human output distributions overlap so closely that source information is effectively absent; or a failure of readout, in which source information is present but the interrogator fails to extract it. To dissociate these possibilities, we inverted the Turing Test, placing humans and AI systems in symmetrical interrogator roles. Using a quantitative information-processing framework, we separated encoding, the information contained in the text; readout, the extraction of that information by an interrogator; and meta-readout, the interrogator's explicit access to the features guiding its own judgments. Across two series of experiments involving more than 3800 human interrogators and two generations of GPT models (GPT-4 and GPT-5), we found a striking dissociation across processing levels and interrogator types. As AI-generated responses became more human-like from GPT-4 to GPT-5, source information became more difficult for human interrogators to extract. GPT-5 interrogators, by contrast, read out source-discriminating information more accurately than GPT-4 interrogators. This improvement, however, came with a specific limitation: GPT-5 explanations only partially aligned with the feature weightings that actually predicted their judgments. Human interrogators showed the complementary pattern, with modest readout alignment but comparatively higher meta-readout alignment. Together, these findings suggest a double dissociation between readout and meta-readout across human and GPT interrogators."
